### Slide 2

Belonging, capacity and joy bring the threads of today together. Keep all three in the picture when you consider whether a change is helping a student flourish.
Belonging means being recognised, included and able to participate as oneself. Capacity asks us to consider what the day costs, what it protects and what helps restore energy. Joy includes interests, connection and authentic expression.
These ideas connect our neurodiversity-affirming lens, our support design and our partnerships. We begin with the person, learn about their experience, and bring student, family, teacher and support staff knowledge into the decisions.
Look again at the support you plan to fade or change from Session Four. It may reduce a barrier to participation. Could it also support belonging or joy? How might it affect what the day asks of the student? Remember the questions from Session Three: what costs, and what lightens?
Academic progress can be part of the picture, but it won't tell us everything about a student's experience. Quiet compliance or doing something without support is not, by itself, evidence of flourishing. Keep returning to the student, and to belonging, capacity and joy, as you judge what your change is making possible.

### Slide 4

Turn to Start, Continue, Reconsider on page twenty-seven. Plan your next day in school. Have your Session Four My students card nearby, especially the line, one way I will fade support.
Under Start, choose one small step you could begin next week. Your agreed fading step may fit here. Under Continue, name something today confirmed you already do well. Give that real attention: the workshop may have put words around a thoughtful practice you already use. Under Reconsider, name an approach you would now look at differently, without turning reflection into criticism of a colleague, family or school.
How will you know it worked? What evidence will show that? These stems may help: I will. The support or demand I am addressing is. I will notice. I will talk with the teacher about. We will review this on.
Keep the action within your role and the teacher's plan. If it changes that plan, the teacher conversation comes first. An action requiring a student to appear less Autistic needs rethinking.
Allow eight minutes. This is your card. The suggested next step is sharing one thing from it with the teacher you work with; anything beyond that is optional. Pause here to write, then return to check the action.

### Slide 4

Let's look once more at your Start. Is it small enough to begin? Does it name a support or demand you can act on within your role? Have you identified what you will discuss with the teacher, how the student’s view will be included, and when you will review what happens together?
Check the evidence you plan to notice. If the measure is simply that the student becomes quieter or complies more readily, look again. What will help you understand their access, participation and experience? Include their perspective in a way that works for them.
Keep the action small, testable and open to revision. Your Continue and Reconsider are useful alongside it: one recognises a practice worth sustaining, and the other keeps your learning open.

### Slide 5

Now plan the next conversation on page twenty-eight. Bring forward your Session Five bridge notes from page twenty-five and the process change from your power-dynamics reflection on page twenty-three.
Choose a real conversation within two weeks, with a student's family or with the student themselves. Write its purpose in one sentence. If it is with a family, plan it with the teacher, or record how the teacher will be involved. You might begin with the warm handover: 'That matters. Can we bring the teacher into this together?'
Plan what you will share, beginning with a strength; what you will ask specifically; and what you will offer. Keep offers within your role. Anything that changes the student's plan needs the teacher involved. Include how the student's communication and preferences will be heard.
Write your opening sentence word for word. Does it invite something you don't yet know? Leave room for the other person's knowledge to change your understanding. Apply your process change to the arrangements, and note when, where and how the conversation will happen.
Use initials or roles only, and share your notes only if you wish. Allow eight minutes. Pause here to make the plan, then resume.
